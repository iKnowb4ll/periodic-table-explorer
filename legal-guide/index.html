Your constitutional protections

1987 Constitution • Article III

Due process & equal protection

The government must follow fair legal procedures and apply the law equally. A person should not be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process.

Protection from unreasonable searches

People have constitutional protection against unreasonable searches and seizures. Warrants generally require probable cause determined by a judge, subject to recognized legal exceptions.

Speech, expression & peaceful assembly

People have protected freedoms to speak, express views, publish, assemble peacefully, and petition the government, subject to lawful limits.

Rights when accused

These include the presumption of innocence, the right to remain silent, the right to competent and independent counsel, and protection against torture or coercion.

Stay on the right side of the law

Legal cautions everyone should know

Do not threaten, stalk, or harass

Repeated unwanted contact, threats, intimidation, harassment, or conduct covered by specific laws can lead to serious consequences. Respect boundaries and stop when asked.

Do not access accounts or devices without permission

Do not break into accounts, guess or steal passwords, bypass access controls, impersonate others, or misuse private data. Unauthorized access and related acts may be crimes.

Do not share private images or personal data

Never distribute intimate images, private messages, addresses, passwords, or identifying information to shame, threaten, or exploit someone. Consent and privacy matter.

Do not carry or use prohibited substances

Drug laws are strict. Avoid unknown substances, offers to carry packages for strangers, or requests to store items for someone else. If pressured, move away and tell a trusted adult.

Do not falsify documents or scam people

Forging records, using another person's identity, fraud, theft, and deceptive schemes can expose people to civil or criminal liability.

Do not forward threats or unverified accusations

Pause before reposting. Defamation, threats, privacy violations, and other online conduct may have legal consequences depending on the facts. Keep evidence and report harmful content.

Protect yourself

Personal safety checklist

Online safety

  • Never share passwords, PINs, or one-time codes—even with someone claiming to be support staff.
  • Keep social profiles private and avoid posting your live location.
  • Do not send private photos or documents to people you have not verified.
  • Save relevant messages and account details if you are being threatened or scammed.

When someone pressures you

  • You can say no and leave a situation that feels unsafe.
  • Do not meet an online stranger alone or get into a vehicle with someone you do not trust.
  • Tell a trusted adult, family member, teacher, or appropriate authority.
  • If immediate danger exists, get to a safe public place and call 911.
You do not have to handle it alone

Where to seek help

Immediate danger

Call 911 or go to a safe place and contact a trusted adult or local authorities.

Legal assistance

Ask the Public Attorney's Office (PAO) or a qualified lawyer about eligibility and the right next steps.

Rights concerns

For human-rights concerns, consult the Commission on Human Rights (CHR) and its official channels.
